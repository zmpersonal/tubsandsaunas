Side-by-side reference

Sauna vs cold plunge vs hot tub.

Similar backyard real estate. Very different infrastructure, maintenance and energy profiles.

Planning factorSaunaCold plungeHot tub
Typical footprint18–60+ sq ft10–20 sq ft35–65+ sq ft
Electrical120V or 240V depending on type/modelOften 120V; larger chillers varyOften 240V; some plug-and-play models use 120V
WaterNone for infrared; traditional sauna may use small amounts on stones where permittedFilled basin + filtration/sanitation systemLarge heated water volume + filtration/sanitation
Drainage planningDepends on model/siteImportantImportant
Energy patternHigh draw, short sessionsLower continuous/periodic chilling loadContinuous heat retention + pumps
Maintenance patternGenerally low to moderateWater quality + filters + chiller maintenanceWater chemistry + filters + pumps + cover
Best space efficiencyInfrared cabinVertical/barrel plungeCompact 2–3 person spa
Featured sourceInHouse Wellness saunasInHouse Wellness cold plungesUse manufacturer/dealer specifications
H
Choose heat when

Efficient footprint + dry ritual

Infrared cabins can be compact and some run on 120V. Larger traditional saunas usually require more electrical and site planning.

C
Choose cold when

The smallest station wins

A plunge can fit where a hot tub cannot, but water handling, sanitation, drainage and chiller ventilation still matter.

S
Choose soaking when

Shared seating is the point

Hot tubs often consume the most area and usually demand the most continuous water and energy management.
